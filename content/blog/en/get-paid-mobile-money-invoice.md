Mobile Money is the default way small businesses get paid across much of Africa — M-Pesa in East Africa, MTN MoMo, Orange Money, Moov and Wave in the West and Centre. Yet most invoices still don't say **how** to pay.

## 1. Put the wallet details on the invoice

A clear block at the bottom: **operator, number, account holder name**. If the name the operator displays during the transfer doesn't match the name on the invoice, the client will hesitate. For M-Pesa, add your **Paybill or Till number** if you have one.

## 2. Ask for a reference

Ask the client to put the **invoice number** in the transfer reason, or to send you the transaction ID. Reconciling ten payments at month-end is impossible without it.

## 3. Give a second option

Add a bank account or a card payment link (Paystack, Flutterwave, Stripe). Some clients — companies, NGOs, foreign clients — can't pay by Mobile Money.

## 4. Beware of fake transfer SMS

The classic scam: a "client" sends a fake confirmation SMS, then asks you to refund an "overpayment". **Always check your wallet balance or the official operator message**, never a forwarded screenshot.

## 5. Send a receipt

Once the money is in, send a receipt. It builds trust and it's the document the client's accountant will ask for.
