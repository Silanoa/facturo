A good invoice is boring on purpose: the client's accountant should find everything in five seconds. Here is what to include.

## The fields every invoice needs

1. Your name or business name, address, email/phone.
2. Your tax or registration number, if any.
3. The client's name and address (and VAT number for EU businesses).
4. A **unique, sequential invoice number** (e.g. INV-2026-0042).
5. Issue date and due date (or payment terms such as "Net 30").
6. Line items: description, quantity, unit price, line total.
7. Subtotal, tax (if applicable) and **total due**, with the currency.
8. How to pay: bank details, payment link, Mobile Money.

## Country-specific lines

- **France (micro-entrepreneur):** *"TVA non applicable, art. 293 B du CGI"* if you are under the VAT threshold, plus the late-payment penalty and the €40 fixed recovery fee for business clients.
- **EU, cross-border B2B:** the client's VAT number and *"Reverse charge"* (Article 196 of the VAT Directive).
- **UK:** if you're not VAT-registered, don't show VAT; if you are, show your VAT number and the VAT amount.
- **US:** no federal VAT; sales tax depends on the state and on what you sell — many services are exempt.
- **Canada:** GST/HST number and the GST/HST amount if you're registered.
- **OHADA countries (Benin, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroon…):** your IFU/NINEA/NIU and RCCM; VAT at the local rate if you are subject to it. Some countries also require certified invoicing systems (e.g. MECeF in Benin, FNE in Côte d'Ivoire) for VAT-registered businesses.

## Numbering

Never reuse or skip numbers. Use a prefix by document type (INV for invoices, QUO for quotes, REC for receipts) and the year.
